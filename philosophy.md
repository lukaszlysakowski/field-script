# Residual Script

*An algorithmic philosophy of marks that remember language without meaning it.*

---

Asemic writing is the skeleton of communication after its content has been removed. The marks remain — the pressure, the sweep, the pause before lifting the pen — but the signification is absent. **Residual Script** approaches this territory computationally: a meticulous, painstaking system in which space itself is recursively remembered, then inscribed with strokes that carry the *shape* of intention without any semantic payload. Each seed generates a complete foreign manuscript. No two are translatable. All are somehow legible.

The spatial memory is built through recursive subdivision with organic drift at every split point — cells of varying scales pressed together like a script whose letter-sizes respond to meaning that no longer exists. A density wave modulated horizontally across the canvas creates the horizontal banding of written text: dense zones where glyphs cluster into something like words or sentences, sparse voids between that suggest line-spacing and paragraph breaks. The system knows nothing of language; it only knows the spatial rhythm language leaves behind.

Within each terminal cell, a position-seeded glyph vocabulary selects one of seven abstract stroke types — uprights, hooks, arches, S-curves, open arcs, crossing loops, diagonal sweeps — each expressed as a cubic bezier curve. The strokes are not letters; they are the *memory* of how letters behave under pressure. Control points are calculated from the cell's geometry so that every glyph belongs to its zone, its scale, its depth in the recursive hierarchy. A meticulously tuned algorithm ensures that deep, small cells produce finer, more intimate marks, while shallow large cells produce heavier, more structural strokes — depth-weighted line weight as a proxy for pen pressure across scales.

The connecting thread is inherited from the source projects' single-stroke pen plotter aesthetic: a thin, semi-transparent trace between each glyph's endpoint and the next's entry point, drawing the hand of the scribe in the air. This thread is the most honest mark in the image — it does not pretend to be a character, only a gesture. In dense regions it is nearly invisible, woven into the glyph density. In transitions between text lines, it grows long and visible, like the return stroke of a calligrapher's hand moving to begin the next row.

Organic wobble — inherited from the Lifeform subdivision system — applies noise-displaced perturbation to all four points of every bezier curve. The wobble is controlled globally, scaling from precise drafting instrument quality to the loose, pressured irregularity of a reed pen on rough paper. This perturbation is the algorithm's deepest acknowledgment that the marks it makes are *physical* — that even abstract writing carries the tremor of a hand. The result, when refined through countless parameter iterations by someone at the absolute top of their field in computational mark-making, feels discovered rather than generated: a manuscript from a civilization that never was, legible in its form and silent in its content.
